\section{Results Discussion}

\subsection{On the Necessity of the Retain Buffer}
\label{8.1}
A natural concern is whether the replay buffer $D_r \subset D_r^{\text{full}}$ contradicts GDPR/CCPA erasure mandates. We argue $D_r$ is unavoidable: pretraining entangles $D_f$ and $D_r^{\text{full}}$ in the same parameters, so disentangling them needs a retained reference absent architectural support (e.g., modular pathways). Existing alternatives fall short: impair-and-repair~\cite{tarun2023fast} still needs $D_r$ for repair, source-free unlearning~\cite{ahmed2025towards} works only for convex/linear models, and all non-convex methods, via Fisher/saliency~\cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming,fan2023salun}, retention losses~\cite{kurmanji2023towards,zhao2024continual}, or continual mechanisms~\cite{shibata2021learning,adhikari2025unlearning,liu2022continual,chatterjee2024unified,huang2025unified}, rely on $D_r$; only generative-task exceptions FLAT~\cite{wang2024llm} and ESD~\cite{gandikota2023erasing} avoid it. CU keeps a minimal $D_r$, at most $10\%$ of $D_r^{\text{full}}$, at least one sample per retain identity, per GDPR/CCPA. \textcolor{red}{Retain metrics use the full held-out split, not $D_r$. $D_r$ holds only \emph{consenting} identities: withdrawal is treated as a forget request, and the buffer refills from the consenting pool.}
