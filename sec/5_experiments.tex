\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\noindent
\textbf{Datasets, Backbones, and Baselines:} We evaluate on CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning} (DeiT-Tiny~\cite{touvron2021training}), CASIA-Iris~\cite{singh2017gender} (LAM~\cite{luo2025novel}), Sokoto Coventry fingerprints~\cite{shehu2018sokoto} (JIPNet~\cite{guan2024joint}), and CASIA-Face100~\cite{yi2014learning} (Face Transformer~\cite{zhong2021face}), comparing CU against SCRUB~\cite{kurmanji2023towards}, SalUn~\cite{fan2023salun}, GS-LoRA~\cite{zhao2024continual}, LTU~\cite{huang2024learning}, MCU~\cite{shi2025mcu}, and Forget Vectors~\cite{sun2024forget}, plus an Oracle trained only on $D_r^{\textbf{full}}$ as the upper bound.

\vspace{0.02cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Evaluation Metrics:} We report $Acc_f$, $Acc_r$, MIA using the label-only attack~\cite{choquette2021label} where $\approx 0.5$ is safe, KL divergence to Oracle, RTE, and H-Mean~\cite{shibata2021learning}. Success requires $Acc_f \approx 0$, $Acc_r \approx$ Oracle, low KL/RTE. FAR@$\{0.3,0.4,0.5\}$/EER, computed via cosine similarity between forget probes and the retain gallery, further verify open-set identity confusion in Section~\ref{sec:attack}.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:main-results}
Table~\ref{tab:all} compares all methods across four tasks, where \colorbox{green!30}{green}/\colorbox{blue!30}{blue} mark best/second-best.

\vspace{0.02cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Results:} All methods forget near-perfectly ($Acc_f < 1\%$). CU ranks first or second in $Acc_r$ and H-Mean across all tasks, with LTU and MCU closest, achieves MIA $\approx 0.5$ despite GS-LoRA's lower KL from deliberately reshaped forget logits, and delivers a 3--4$\times$ RTE speedup over every baseline.

\subsection{Attack-Based Verification}
\label{sec:attack}
\vspace{0.02cm}
\noindent
Table~\ref{tab:far_eer} reports FAR/EER of forget probes vs.\ the retain gallery on CASIA-Iris. CU tracks the Oracle closely, with FAR@0.5 at $3.83\%$ vs.\ $3.10\%$ and EER at $21.99\%$ vs.\ $21.69\%$, and both rise similarly over \textit{Before} at $1.64\%$, so the small increase stems from removing identities, not from CU.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\small
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}

\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Model & FAR@0.3 & FAR@0.4 & FAR@0.5 & EER \\
\midrule
Before & 11.38\% & 4.84\% & 1.64\% & 13.33\% \\
\rowcolor{orange!10}
CU & 17.97\% & 8.52\% & 3.83\% & 21.99\% \\
Oracle & 16.10\% & 7.79\% & 3.10\% & 21.69\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{0.03cm}
\caption{FAR/EER of forget probes vs.\ retain gallery on CASIA-Iris.}
\label{tab:far_eer}
\end{table}

\vspace{0.02cm}
\noindent
Aggregate FAR/EER cannot rule out a concentrated, backdoor-like confound between one forget identity and its assigned nearest-retain centroid, since such a pairing barely moves the aggregate rate. Table~\ref{tab:assigned_centroid} reports this per-identity measurement on CASIA-Face100 against the Oracle, with match rates in \% at the same cosine-similarity threshold as FAR@0.5 in Table~\ref{tab:far_eer}, covering assigned-centroid match, linear probe, relearning, MIA, and model inversion. CU's assigned-centroid match rate is no higher than Oracle's, ruling out concentrated confounding between individual forget--retain pairs. Its linear-probe rate stays within a point of Oracle, relearning is slow, and label-only/adaptive MIA plus inversion match sit near chance, showing forgetting is diffuse and adaptive attacks also fail.
