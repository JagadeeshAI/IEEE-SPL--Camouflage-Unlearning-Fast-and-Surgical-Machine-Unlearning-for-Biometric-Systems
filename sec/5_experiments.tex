\section{Performance Evaluation}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\noindent
\textbf{Datasets and Backbones:} To comprehensively evaluate the proposed CU, we examine its performance across four distinct tasks. We begin with image classification on CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning}, employing the DeiT-Tiny model~\cite{touvron2021training}. Extending our evaluation to biometric authentication, we examine iris recognition using CASIA-Iris~\cite{singh2017gender} with the lightweight LAM architecture~\cite{luo2025novel}. For fingerprint verification, we leverage the Sokoto Coventry dataset~\cite{shehu2018sokoto} paired with JIPNet~\cite{guan2024joint}, a feature learning approach that eliminates hand-crafted descriptors. Our evaluation concludes with face recognition on CASIA-Face100~\cite{yi2014learning} where we deploy the Face Transformer~\cite{zhong2021face} as our recognition backbone.

\noindent \textbf{Baselines:} To establish a comprehensive benchmark, we compare against established and recent machine unlearning methods. From foundational approaches, we implement SCRUB~\cite{kurmanji2023towards}, a student-teacher framework with bidirectional logit manipulation; SalUn~\cite{fan2023salun}, which employs gradient-based weight saliency; and GS-LoRA~\cite{zhao2024continual}, utilizing group sparse regularization with LoRA adapters. We further evaluate emerging methods: LTU~\cite{huang2024learning}, applying meta-learning with gradient harmonization; MCU~\cite{shi2025mcu}, leveraging B\'ezier curve optimization in parameter space; and Forget Vectors~\cite{sun2024forget}, which perturbs inputs to suppress forget class predictions. All methods are evaluated against an Oracle baseline, defined as a model trained exclusively on $D_r^{\textbf{full}}$, representing the best achievable performance on retain classes. This provides a rigorous evaluation framework for our proposed CU method.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Evaluation Metrics:} We evaluate forget accuracy $Acc_f$, retain accuracy $Acc_r$, membership inference attack success (MIA), KL divergence, and runtime efficiency (RTE), measured in seconds. Following Shibata \textit{et al.}~\cite{shibata2021learning}, we use H-mean as $H\text{-}Mean = \frac{2 \cdot Acc_r \cdot (1 - Acc_f)}{Acc_r + (1 - Acc_f)}$. Successful unlearning requires $Acc_f \approx 0$, MIA $\approx 0.5$ indicates near-random membership inference, low KL divergence and RTE, with $Acc_r$ near oracle performance. \textcolor{red}{MIA follows the label-only attack of~\cite{choquette2021label}, combining gap, boundary-distance, and augmentation attacks, and reports attack accuracy on member (forget-train) versus non-member data, where $0.5$ indicates the attacker cannot infer membership. KL divergence is reported as $\mathrm{KL}(\mathcal{M}_{\text{unlearned}} \,\|\, \mathcal{M}_{\text{oracle}})$ over the evaluation set, measuring proximity of the unlearned model to the oracle.}

\textcolor{red}{Because class-attribution accuracy alone does not capture the open-set, embedding-distance-thresholding nature of real-world biometric verification, we additionally report False Accept Rate (FAR) at thresholds $0.3$, $0.4$, and $0.5$, and Equal Error Rate (EER), computed by matching forget-class probes against the retain gallery under cosine similarity. Low FAR/EER close to the Oracle indicates that removed identities are not confused with any retained identity beyond chance level.}

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Implementation Details:} All experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA RTX A4000 GPU using PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch} and Python 3.11. Unless otherwise specified, we set $\lambda_{\textit{dom}} = 0.1$, $\lambda_{\textit{ce}} = 0.4$, $\lambda_{\textit{emb}} = 1.0$, and $\lambda_{\textit{local}} = 0.01$, with margin $\gamma = 2.0$. All baselines and CU are fine-tuned for 10 epochs using SGD~\cite{robbins1951stochastic} with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$, momentum of 0.9, and batch size of 64.

\subsection{Results Discussion}
Table~\ref{tab:all} presents results across four tasks: classification, iris recognition, fingerprint recognition, and face recognition, where \colorbox{green!30}{green} highlights best performance and \colorbox{blue!30}{blue} indicates second-best.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Unlearning and Retention:} All methods achieve near perfect forgetting $Acc_f < 1\%$, approaching random assignment. Proposed CU consistently ranks first or second in $Acc_r$ across all tasks, with LTU as the strongest competitor on classification, iris, and fingerprint tasks, while MCU excels on iris recognition. For \textit{H-Mean}, proposed CU alternates between first and second positions alongside competitive showings from LTU, FV, MCU, and GS-LoRA.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Privacy and Efficiency:} GS-LoRA achieves lower KL divergence than CU, which is expected since CU deliberately modifies logit distributions to suppress forget classes and is therefore less competitive on this metric. However, MIA scores near 0.5 confirm CU provides comparable privacy protection. Most critically, proposed CU achieves 3 to 4$\bm{\times}$ speedup versus all baselines, validating our design objective: fast, privacy-preserving unlearning without catastrophic forgetting.

\noindent
\textbf{Geometric Analysis of Unlearning Behavior:}
\label{sec:tsne}
To understand geometric behavior across unlearning methods, we visualize embedding space transformations using t-SNE. Figure~\ref{fig:tsne_comparison} compares baseline methods against proposed CU. Existing approaches GS-LoRA~\cite{zhao2024continual}, SCRUB~\cite{kurmanji2023towards}, and Boundary Unlearning~\cite{chen2023boundary} collapse forget classes into a single distant point, requiring long distance traversal in feature space. CU distributes forget samples across nearest retain class boundaries, achieving faster convergence through shorter migration paths.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Retain Dominance Constraint:} Without this constraint, we found that the forget accuracy exhibits a prolonged tail, delaying convergence. Table~\ref{tab:dominance_ablation} demonstrates that applying the constraint reduces $Acc_f$ from 9.97\% to 0.39\%, achieving faster unlearning with minimal retention loss.
